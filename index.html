Not All Positions Age the Same
Each dot represents the mean career age for players at that position, with error bars showing how wide the spread is. Quarterbacks peak later. Running backs peak youngest and fall off fastest. 26.3 avg peak age for running backs
Chapter 2
When the Drop Off Hits
Performance doesn't fade gradually. It falls off a cliff. Watch for the inflection point on the curve: the age where year over year decline accelerates sharply. For running backs it comes early. For quarterbacks, that cliff is a decade away. 28 age where RB decline sharply accelerates
Why It Matters
The Body Keeps the Score
Running backs absorb more hits per carry than any other skill position. Wide receivers face constant contact at the catch point. Linemen battle in the trenches every single snap. The wear compounds and the data reflects it in cold, precise numbers.
The Business of Age
Contracts Follow the Curve
Teams don't just watch film. They watch the data. A running back approaching 28 becomes a liability on the cap sheet before he becomes one on the field. The market has already priced in the decline before the player even feels it.
Context
The Numbers Behind the Narrative
Every position has its own aging curve shaped by the physical demands of the role. The data doesn't lie about who gets replaced and when.
Chapter 3
Tom Brady Played in a Different Universe
The average quarterback retires around 36. Brady retired at 45, then unretired, then retired again. His career doesn't just sit outside the norm. It redefines what the upper boundary of the chart even means. 45 Brady's retirement age vs ~36 avg for QBs
Chapter 4
Replaceable at 24. Irreplaceable at 38.
For running backs, teams can find a replacement cheaply and quickly, which is why veteran running backs rarely get second contracts. For quarterbacks, the replacement cost is enormous, which is why teams extend aging quarterbacks long past the point other positions retire. The performance curve and the replacement curve tell the full story.
Fin
The Clock Always Runs Out
Whether at 24 or 45, every NFL career ends the same way. The data just tells you roughly when to expect it and who gets to defy it.
